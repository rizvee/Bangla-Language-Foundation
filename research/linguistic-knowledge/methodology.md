# Linguistic Knowledge Extraction & Modeling Methodology — BLF

## 1. Objective & Architectural Positioning
The BLF Linguistic Knowledge Layer serves as the rigorous intermediate representation between verified primary grammatical literature (`sources/registry/sources.json`) and downstream computational models / future dataset realization.

Directly converting text from copyrighted or traditional grammar books into mass training corpora is strictly barred. Instead, grammar sources are systematically decomposed into:
```
SOURCE RECORD (sources.json)
  └──> LINGUISTIC EVIDENCE (locators, excerpts, page/section references)
        └──> ATOMIC LINGUISTIC CLAIM (independently verifiable proposition)
              └──> NORMALIZED INTERPRETATION (BLF canonical terminology)
                    └──> MACHINE-READABLE RULE (declarative template, constraints)
                          └──> PROVENANCE-TRACKED EXAMPLE (source or validated fixture)
```

---

## 2. Epistemic Classification Framework
Every linguistic proposition in BLF is explicitly classified into one of four epistemic tiers:

1. `SOURCE_ASSERTED`: Directly attested and asserted in a verified primary source with unambiguous locator.
2. `BLF_NORMALIZED`: Direct source claim represented using standardized modern linguistic terminology.
3. `BLF_INFERRED`: Logically necessary structural inference derived from multiple attested patterns, but not stated verbatim in a single text.
4. `BLF_HYPOTHESIS`: Theoretical construction requiring empirical cross-corpus or experimental validation.

**Guardrail Rule**: Only `SOURCE_ASSERTED` and reviewed `BLF_NORMALIZED` propositions may support `GOLD` tier generative rules.

---

## 3. Human Review & Verification States
Knowledge items transition through formal workflow states:
- `AUTO_EXTRACTED`: Candidate extraction generated by automated parsers or initial drafting.
- `SOURCE_VERIFIED`: Verified against primary source text and confirmed by automated integrity checkers.
- `LINGUIST_REVIEW_REQUIRED`: Disputed, edge-case, or cross-framework conflict flagged for human review.
- `HUMAN_APPROVED`: Explicitly signed off by an authenticated human linguist. *(Automated pipelines are strictly barred from assigning this status).*
- `REJECTED`: Falsified or erroneous proposition quarantined in the audit log.
- `SUPERSEDED`: Historical analysis replaced by a more precise contemporary formulation.

---

## 4. Conflict Preservation Discipline
BLF does not homogenize conflicting linguistic frameworks into an artificial consensus. When traditional Bangla Academy grammars, generative syntactic analyses, and descriptive typological models diverge (e.g. 7-case Sanskrit vs 4-case structural morphology), BLF records the divergence explicitly via `RuleRelation` mappings (`SUPPORTS`, `REFINES`, `CONTRADICTS`, `LIMITS`, `SUPERSEDES`).

---

## 5. Scope & Register Boundaries
Every rule and claim must explicitly declare its geographic variety and stylistic register:
- **Canonical Variety**: Bangladesh Standard Bangla (`BDSB_STANDARD`, `BDSB_FORMAL`, `BDSB_COLLOQUIAL`).
- **Stylistic Registers**: `formal_standard`, `colloquial_standard` (Cholit), `intimate_conversational`, `archaic_sadhu`.
- **Dialect Isolation**: Dialectal markers (e.g. Sylheti, Chittagonian) are partitioned in separate namespaces and never conflated with standard BDSB realizations.
